# Chapter 30: Cryospheric and Subglacial / Sub-Ice-Shelf Bed Topography

*Mapping the shifting ice surfaces of the poles and mountain glaciers—and the hidden bedrock continents beneath kilometers of ice.*

---

## 30.7 Curated Key References

The mathematical modeling of polar ice sheet elevation, subglacial bed topography, and sub-ice-shelf ocean cavities represents a landmark synthesis of glaciological continuum mechanics, spaceborne stereophotogrammetry, and airborne radar geophysics. Below are the foundational peer-reviewed scientific papers, dataset specifications, and algorithmic benchmarks.

---

### Subglacial Bed Topography and Mass Conservation

1. **Morlighem, M., Williams, C. N., Rignot, E., An, L., Arndt, J. E., Bamber, J. L., ... & Zinglersen, K. B. (2017).** *BedMachine v3: Complete bed topography and ocean bathymetry mapping of Greenland from multibeam echo sounding combined with mass conservation.* Geophysical Research Letters, 44(21), 11051–11061. DOI: [10.1002/2017GL074954](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017GL074954).
   * Seminal paper deriving the 2D mass conservation inversion equation and demonstrating the elimination of artificial Kriged bedrock dams across Greenland's marine-terminating glaciers.

2. **Morlighem, M., Rignot, E., Binder, T., Blankenship, D., Casassa, R., Cui, X., ... & Young, D. A. (2020).** *Deep glacial troughs and stabilizing ridges unveiled beneath the margins of the Antarctic ice sheet.* Nature Geoscience, 13(2), 132–137. DOI: [10.1038/s41561-019-0510-8](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-019-0510-8).
   * Documents BedMachine Antarctica, compiling 1.5 million line-kilometers of radar soundings and discovering Earth's deepest subglacial canyon beneath Denman Glacier ($3,500\text{ m}$ below sea level).

3. **Fretwell, P., Pritchard, H. D., Vaughan, D. G., Bamber, J. L., Barrand, N. E., Bell, R., ... & Zirizzotti, A. (2013).** *Bedmap2: improved ice bed, surface and thickness datasets for Antarctica.* The Cryosphere, 7(1), 375–393. DOI: [10.5194/tc-7-375-2013](https://doi.org/10.5194/tc-7-375-2013).
   * The benchmark compilation of continental Antarctic ice thickness and bedrock topography prior to mass-conservation inversion, detailing airborne radio-echo sounding coverage.

---

### High-Resolution Polar Surface Models and Co-Registration

4. **Howat, I. M., Porter, C., Smith, B. E., Noh, M. J., & Morin, P. (2019).** *The Reference Elevation Model of Antarctica.* The Cryosphere, 13(2), 665–674. DOI: [10.5194/tc-13-665-2019](https://doi.org/10.5194/tc-13-665-2019).
   * Documents the creation of REMA, detailing petascale stereophotogrammetry from WorldView imagery, SETSM processing, and ICESat laser altimetry co-registration.

5. **Noh, M. J., & Howat, I. M. (2015).** *Automated stereo-photogrammetric DEM generation at high latitudes: Surface Extraction with TIN-based Search-space Minimization (SETSM) validation and demonstration over glaciated terrain.* GIScience & Remote Sensing, 52(2), 198–217. DOI: [10.1080/15481603.2015.1008621](https://doi.org/10.1080/15481603.2015.1008621).
   * The foundational algorithmic paper for SETSM, proving robust, fully automated 3D surface reconstruction over low-contrast polar snowfields without manual ground control.

6. **Nuth, C., & Kääb, A. (2011).** *Co-registration and bias corrections of satellite elevation data sets for quantifying glacier thickness change.* The Cryosphere, 5(1), 271–290. DOI: [10.5194/tc-5-271-2011](https://doi.org/10.5194/tc-5-271-2011).
   * Landmark analytical formulation for 3D DEM co-registration over stable non-glaciated terrain, solving for sub-pixel horizontal shifts, elevation-dependent biases, and aspect-correlated errors.

7. **Smith, B., Fricker, H. A., Holschuh, N., Gardner, A. S., Adusumilli, S., Brunt, K. M., ... & Zwally, H. J. (2019).** *Land ice height-retrieval algorithm for NASA's ICESat-2 photon-counting laser altimeter.* Remote Sensing of Environment, 233, 111352. DOI: [10.1016/j.rse.2019.111352](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.rse.2019.111352).
   * Derives the ATL06 land-ice elevation and slope retrieval algorithm for ICESat-2's six-beam 532 nm photon-counting ATLAS instrument.

8. **Hugonnet, R., McNabb, R., Berthier, E., Menounos, B., Nuth, C., Girod, L., ... & Kääb, A. (2021).** *Accelerated global glacier mass loss in the early twenty-first century.* Nature, 592(7856), 726–731. DOI: [10.1038/s41586-021-03436-z](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03436-z).
   * Global multi-temporal stereo DEM analysis (`xdem` framework) quantifying the elevation change and mass balance of all Earth's mountain glaciers with spatially correlated error propagation.

### Ice Sheet Modeling, Hydrostatics, and Radar Firn Penetration

9. **Larour, E., Seroussi, H., Morlighem, M., & Rignot, E. (2012).** *Continental scale, high order, high spatial resolution, ice sheet modeling using the Ice Sheet System Model (ISSM).* Journal of Geophysical Research: Earth Surface, 117(F1), F01022. DOI: [10.1029/2011JF002140](https://doi.org/10.1029/2011JF002140).
   * Foundational reference for the open-source finite-element Ice Sheet System Model (ISSM) that powers BedMachine mass-conservation inversions.

10. **Schoof, C. (2007).** *Ice sheet grounding line dynamics: Steady states, stability, and hysteresis.* Journal of Geophysical Research: Earth Surface, 112(F3), F03S28. DOI: [10.1029/2006JF000664](https://doi.org/10.1029/2006JF000664).
    * Mathematical proof of Marine Ice Sheet Instability (MISI), showing why retrograde subglacial bed slopes ($\nabla z_{\text{bed}}$ sloping downward inland) drive runaway grounding-line retreat.

11. **Griggs, J. A., & Bamber, J. L. (2011).** *Antarctic ice-shelf thickness from satellite radar altimetry.* Journal of Glaciology, 57(203), 485–498. DOI: [10.3189/002214311796905659](https://doi.org/10.3189/002214311796905659).
    * Exhaustive formulation of hydrostatic buoyancy equilibrium for floating ice shelves, deriving the sensitivity of ice thickness to firn air content ($h_{\text{air}}$) and seawater density.

12. **Rignot, E., Echelmeyer, K., & Krabill, W. (2001).** *Penetration depth of interferometric synthetic-aperture radar signals in snow and ice.* Geophysical Research Letters, 28(18), 3501–3504. DOI: [10.1029/2000GL012484](https://doi.org/10.1029/2000GL012484).
    * Quantifies multi-meter microwave radar penetration depths into dry polar firn and glacier ice by comparing C-/L-band InSAR phase centers against airborne laser altimetry.

13. **Fricker, H. A., Popov, S., Allison, I., & Young, N. (2001).** *Distribution of marine ice beneath the Amery Ice Shelf.* Geophysical Research Letters, 28(11), 2241–2244. DOI: [10.1029/2000GL012461](https://doi.org/10.1029/2000GL012461).
    * Combines satellite radar altimeter hydrostatic inversions with airborne radio-echo sounding to map basal marine ice accretion where radio-echo sounding is absorbed by saline ice.

14. **Nilsson, J., Vallelonga, P., Simonsen, S. B., Sørensen, L. S., Forsberg, R., Dahl-Jensen, D., ... & Satow, K. (2015).** *Greenland 2012 melt event effects on CryoSat-2 radar altimetry.* Geophysical Research Letters, 42(10), 3919–3926. DOI: [10.1002/2015GL063296](https://doi.org/10.1002/2015GL063296).
    * Demonstrates how summer meltwater refreezing creates a shallow internal ice lens in Greenland firn, causing the Ku-band CryoSat-2 radar scattering horizon to jump meters upward overnight.
