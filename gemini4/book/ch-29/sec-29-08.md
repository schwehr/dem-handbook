# Chapter 29: Fluid Mud, "Nautical Depth," and Multi-Horizon Subsurface DEMs

*When the seabed or ground surface is not a sharp boundary: modeling rheologic transitions, fluid mud, and stacked subsurface horizons.*

---

## 29.8 Curated Key References

The management of estuarine fluid mud, the standardization of rheological nautical depth, and the mathematical modeling of multi-horizon stratigraphic systems represent a crucial intersection of cohesive sediment transport, marine acoustics, and structural geostatistics. Below are the foundational peer-reviewed scientific papers, engineering standards, and historical works.

---

### Nautical Depth and Estuarine Fluid Mud

1. **World Association for Waterborne Transport Infrastructure (PIANC). (2014).** *Harbour Approach Channels—Design Guidelines (Report No. 121).* PIANC General Secretariat, Brussels, Belgium. ISBN: [978-2872232109](https://isbnsearch.org/isbn/9782872232109).
   * The definitive international engineering standard establishing operational guidelines for commercial ship transit through fluid mud, formally defining the bulk density ($\rho = 1200\text{ kg/m}^3$) and yield stress ($\tau_y = 70\text{–}100\text{ Pa}$) criteria for Nautical Depth.

2. **McAnally, W. H., Friedrichs, C., Hamilton, D., Hayter, E., Shrestha, P., Rodriguez, H., ... & Teeter, A. (2007).** *Management of fluid mud in estuaries, bays, and lakes. I: Present state of understanding on character and behavior.* Journal of Hydraulic Engineering, 133(1), 9–22. DOI: [10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9429(2007)133:1(9)](https://doi.org/10.1061/%28ASCE%290733-9429%282007%29133%3A1%289%29).
   * Comprehensive scientific review of cohesive sediment dynamics, flocculation mechanics, estuarine turbidity maxima, non-Newtonian Bingham rheology, and acoustic detection.

3. **McAnally, W. H., Teeter, A., Schoellhamer, D., Friedrichs, C., Hamilton, D., Hayter, E., ... & Kirby, R. (2007).** *Management of fluid mud in estuaries, bays, and lakes. II: Measurement, modeling, and management.* Journal of Hydraulic Engineering, 133(1), 23–38. DOI: [10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9429(2007)133:1(23)](https://doi.org/10.1061/%28ASCE%290733-9429%282007%29133%3A1%2823%29).
   * Companion ASCE task committee synthesis evaluating dual-frequency echosounders, nuclear/tuning-fork densitometers, rheological yield-stress probes, and nautical depth maintenance strategies.

4. **Carneiro, J. C., Gallo, M. N., & Vinzón, S. B. (2020).** *Detection of fluid mud layers using tuning fork, dual-frequency echo sounder, and chirp sub-bottom measurements.* Ocean Dynamics, 70(4), 573–590. DOI: [10.1007/s10236-020-01346-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10236-020-01346-8).
   * Demonstrates integrated multi-sensor field campaigns combining resonant tuning-fork rheometry, dual-frequency echosounding ($200/33\text{ kHz}$), and chirp sub-bottom profiling to map estuarine fluid-mud horizons.

5. **Toorman, E. A. (1997).** *Modelling the thixotropic behaviour of dense cohesive sediment suspensions.* Rheologica Acta, 36(1), 56–65. DOI: [10.1007/BF00366724](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00366724).
   * Formulates the structural kinetic differential equation governing thixotropic yield stress and shear-history dependence in estuarine fluid mud.

6. **Wurpts, R., & Torn, P. (2005).** *15 years experience with fluid mud: Definition of the nautical bottom with rheological parameters.* Terra et Aqua, 99, 22–32.
   * Authoritative operational review detailing long-term sea trials in the Port of Emden and European estuaries, proving that vessels safely navigate through fluid mud when steered relative to a $70\text{–}100\text{ Pa}$ rheological yield stress threshold.

7. **Kirby, R., Parker, W. R., & van Oostrum, W. H. A. (1980).** *Definition of the seabed in navigation routes through mud areas.* The International Hydrographic Review, 57(1), 107–117.
   * Landmark historical paper introducing the concept of the "nautical bottom" ($\rho = 1200\text{ kg/m}^3$ in Rotterdam/Europort) to the international hydrographic community and exposing the frequency-depth discrepancy between $200\text{-kHz}$ and $30\text{-kHz}$ sounders.

### Subsurface Stratigraphy, Implicit Modeling, and Geoacoustics

8. **Heezen, B. C., & Ewing, M. (1952).** *Turbidity currents and submarine slumps, and the 1929 Grand Banks [Newfoundland] earthquake.* American Journal of Science, 250(12), 849–873. DOI: [10.2475/ajs.250.12.849](https://doi.org/10.2475/ajs.250.12.849).
   * Foundational marine geology paper proving that dense sediment-laden turbidity flows travel hundreds of kilometers across continental slopes, establishing the physical basis of submarine sediment-gravity flows.

9. **Hamilton, E. L. (1980).** *Geoacoustic modeling of the sea floor.* The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America, 68(5), 1313–1340. DOI: [10.1121/1.385100](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.385100).
   * Classic reference establishing empirical and physical relationships between marine sediment bulk density, porosity, compressional/shear wave velocities, and frequency-dependent acoustic attenuation.

10. **Buckingham, M. J. (2000).** *Wave propagation, stress relaxation, and grain-to-grain shearing in saturated, unconsolidated marine sediments.* The Journal of the Acoustical Society of America, 108(6), 2796–2815. DOI: [10.1121/1.1322018](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1322018).
    * Derives the dispersion and attenuation scaling laws for compressional and shear waves in unconsolidated marine muds and sands.

11. **Wunderlich, J., Wendt, G., & Müller, S. (2005).** *High-resolution echo-sounding and detection of embedded archaeological objects with nonlinear sub-bottom profilers.* Marine Geophysical Researches, 26(2–4), 123–133. DOI: [10.1007/s11001-005-3712-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11001-005-3712-y).
    * Details the physical principles and shallow-water field performance of non-linear parametric sub-bottom profilers (Innomar SES), demonstrating narrow-beam resolution of upper sediment and fluid-mud horizons without side-lobe clutter.

12. **Smith, W. H. F., & Wessel, P. (1990).** *Gridding with continuous curvature splines in tension.* Geophysics, 55(3), 293–305. DOI: [10.1190/1.1442837](https://doi.org/10.1190/1.1442837).
    * Derives the biharmonic spline-in-tension algorithm implemented in GMT `surface`, including lower and upper bounding-surface constraints (`-Ll`, `-Lu`) for non-crossing multi-horizon interpolation.

13. **Lajaunie, C., Courrioux, G., & Manuel, L. (1997).** *Foliation fields and 3D cartography in geology: Principles of a method based on potential interpolation.* Mathematical Geology, 29(4), 571–584. DOI: [10.1007/BF02775087](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02775087).
    * Seminal mathematical paper introducing 3D potential-field universal co-kriging of geological contacts and structural orientations to guarantee non-crossing stratigraphic isosurfaces.

14. **Calcagno, P., Chilès, J. P., Courrioux, G., & Guillen, A. (2008).** *Geological modelling from field data and geological knowledge: Part I. Modelling method coupling 3D potential-field interpolation and geological rules.* Physics of the Earth and Planetary Interiors, 171(1–4), 147–157. DOI: [10.1016/j.pepi.2008.06.013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pepi.2008.06.013).
    * Formalizes the Stratigraphic Pile (`Erode` vs. `Onlap` unconformity rules) coupling potential-field co-kriging with structural geology topology.

15. **de la Varga, M., Schaaf, A., & Wellmann, F. (2019).** *GemPy 1.0: open-source stochastic geological modeling and inversion.* Geoscientific Model Development, 12(1), 1–32. DOI: [10.5194/gmd-12-1-2019](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-12-1-2019).
    * Primary citation for GemPy, deriving the open-source Python potential-field framework that guarantees non-crossing topological integrity and uncertainty quantification across multi-horizon geological surfaces.
