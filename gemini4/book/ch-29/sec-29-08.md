# Chapter 29: Fluid Mud, "Nautical Depth," and Multi-Horizon Subsurface DEMs

*When the seabed or ground surface is not a sharp boundary: modeling rheologic transitions, fluid mud, and stacked subsurface horizons.*

---

## 29.8 Curated Key References

The management of estuarine fluid mud, the standardization of rheological nautical depth, and the mathematical modeling of multi-horizon stratigraphic systems represent a crucial intersection of cohesive sediment transport, marine acoustics, and structural geostatistics. Below are the foundational peer-reviewed scientific papers, engineering standards, and historical works.

---

### Nautical Depth and Estuarine Fluid Mud

1. **World Association for Waterborne Transport Infrastructure (PIANC). (2014).** *Harbour Approach Channels—Design Guidelines (Report No. 121).* PIANC General Secretariat, Brussels, Belgium. ISBN: [978-2872232109](https://isbnsearch.org/isbn/9782872232109).
   * The definitive international engineering standard establishing operational guidelines for commercial ship transit through fluid mud, formally defining the bulk density ($\rho = 1200\text{ kg/m}^3$) and yield stress ($\tau_y = 70\text{–}100\text{ Pa}$) criteria for Nautical Depth.

2. **McAnally, W. H., Friedrichs, C., Hamilton, D., Hayter, E., Shrestha, P., Rodriguez, H., ... & Teeter, A. (2007).** *Management of fluid mud in estuaries, bays, and lakes. I: Present state of understanding on character and behavior.* Journal of Hydraulic Engineering, 133(1), 9–22. DOI: [10.1061/(ASCE)0733-9429(2007)133:1(9)](https://doi.org/10.1061/%28ASCE%290733-9429%282007%29133%3A1%289%29).
   * Comprehensive scientific review of cohesive sediment dynamics, flocculation mechanics, estuarine turbidity maxima, non-Newtonian Bingham rheology, and acoustic detection.

3. **Wurpts, R. (2005).** *15 years experience with fluid mud: Definition of the nautical bottom with rheological parameters.* Terra et Aqua, 99, 22–32.
   * Authoritative operational review detailing long-term sea trials in German and Dutch estuaries (Ems, Weser, Rotterdam), proving that vessels safely navigate through high-density fluid mud when steered relative to rheological yield limits.

4. **Kirby, R., Parker, W. R., & van Oostrum, W. H. A. (1980).** *Definition of the seabed in navigation routes through mud areas.* The International Hydrographic Review, 57(1), 107–117.
   * Landmark historical paper introducing the concept of the "nautical bottom" to the international hydrographic community, exposing the frequency-depth discrepancy between $200\text{-kHz}$ and $30\text{-kHz}$ sounders.

### Subsurface Stratigraphy and Sub-Bottom Acoustics

5. **Heezen, B. C., & Ewing, M. (1952).** *Turbidity currents and submarine slumps, and the 1929 Grand Banks [Newfoundland] earthquake.* American Journal of Science, 250(12), 849–873. DOI: [10.2475/ajs.250.12.849](https://doi.org/10.2475/ajs.250.12.849).
   * Foundational marine geology paper proving that dense sediment-laden turbidity flows travel hundreds of kilometers across continental slopes, establishing the physical basis of deep-water fluid mud dynamics.

6. **Wunderlich, J., & Wendt, G. (2000).** *High-resolution parametric sub-bottom profiler for shallow waters.* In *Proceedings of the 5th European Conference on Underwater Acoustics (ECUA 2000)* (pp. 133–140). Lyon, France.
   * Details the physical principles and field performance of non-linear parametric acoustic sounders (Innomar SES), demonstrating narrow-beam resolution of estuarine density horizons without side-lobes.

7. **de la Varga, M., Schaaf, A., & Wellmann, F. (2019).** *GemPy 1.0: open-source stochastic geological modeling and inversion.* Geoscientific Model Development, 12(1), 1–32. DOI: [10.5194/gmd-12-1-2019](https://doi.org/10.5194/gmd-12-1-2019).
   * Primary citation for GemPy, deriving the potential-field universal co-Kriging framework that guarantees non-crossing topological integrity across complex multi-horizon geological surfaces.
