# Chapter 25: Deep Dive into Visualization of DEMs

*How human visual perception, colormaps, shading physics, 3D graphics engines, and 3D Gaussian Splats reveal—or distort—terrain and uncertainty.*

---

## 25.9 Curated Key References

The scientific visualization of digital elevation models bridges visual psychophysics, cartographic tradition, differential geometry, and real-time GPU rendering. The references below constitute the foundational scientific, computational, and cartographic works underpinning modern terrain display.

---

### Perception Science and Color Theory

1. **Ware, C. (2020).** *Information Visualization: Perception for Design* (4th ed.). Morgan Kaufmann, Cambridge, MA. ISBN: [978-0128128275](https://isbnsearch.org/isbn/9780128128275).
   * The definitive textbook on visual psychophysics and data display. Codifies the dual-stream theory of vision (magnocellular luminance pathway carrying 3D shape-from-shading vs. parvocellular chromatic pathway carrying categorical grouping).

2. **Crameri, F., Shephard, G. E., & Heron, P. J. (2020).** *The misuse of colour in science communication.* Nature Communications, 11(1), 5444. DOI: [10.1038/s41467-020-19160-7](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-020-19160-7).
   * Landmark open-access paper detailing how rainbow and non-uniform colormaps corrupt scientific data, introduce false Mach-band anomalies, and exclude color-vision deficient individuals. Introduces the `batlow` scientific palette.

3. **Thyng, K. M., Greene, C. A., Hetland, R. D., Zimmerle, H. M., & DiMarco, S. F. (2016).** *True colors of oceanography: Guidelines for effective and accurate colormap selection.* Oceanography, 29(3), 9–13. DOI: [10.5670/oceanog.2016.66](https://doi.org/10.5670/oceanog.2016.66).
   * Documents the design, mathematical formulation, and open-source release of the `cmocean` scientific colormap suite for bathymetry, topography, and oceanographic fields.

4. **Borland, D., & Taylor, R. M. (2007).** *Rainbow color map (still) considered harmful.* IEEE Computer Graphics and Applications, 27(2), 14–17. DOI: [10.1109/MCG.2007.32](https://doi.org/10.1109/MCG.2007.32).
   * Highly influential review dismantling the continued use of rainbow palettes in 2D and 3D scientific visualization.

---

### Analytical Shading, Relief Enhancement, and Cartography

5. **Horn, B. K. (1981).** *Hill shading and the reflectance map.* Proceedings of the IEEE, 69(1), 14–47. DOI: [10.1109/PROC.1981.11918](https://doi.org/10.1109/PROC.1981.11918).
   * Foundational paper deriving analytical shaded relief, surface normal vectors, and the distance-weighted $3\times 3$ finite-difference gradient operator used in almost all GIS software today.

6. **Zakšek, K., Oštir, K., & Kokalj, Ž. (2011).** *Sky-view factor as a relief visualization technique.* Remote Sensing, 3(2), 398–415. DOI: [10.3390/rs3020398](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs3020398).
   * Primary mathematical derivation and operational implementation of the Sky-View Factor (SVF) for direction-independent diffuse illumination of elevation models.

7. **Yokoyama, R., Shirasawa, M., & Pike, R. J. (2002).** *Visualizing topography by openness: A new approach to the representation of digital elevation.* Photogrammetric Engineering & Remote Sensing, 68(3), 257–266.
   * Derives positive and negative topographic openness from multi-directional horizon angles, separating convex ridges from concave gullies without directional lighting bias.

8. **Hesse, R. (2010).** *LiDAR-derived Local Relief Models–a new tool for archaeological prospection.* Archaeological Prospection, 17(2), 67–72. DOI: [10.1002/arp.374](https://doi.org/10.1002/arp.374).
   * Originates the Local Relief Model (LRM) methodology, subtracting low-pass trend surfaces from high-resolution DEMs to reveal subtle micro-topography.

9. **Imhof, E. (1982).** *Cartographic Relief Presentation* (H. J. Steward, Ed.). Walter de Gruyter, Berlin/New York. ISBN: [978-3110067118](https://isbnsearch.org/isbn/9783110067118).
   * The definitive masterwork on manual and theoretical shaded relief cartography, aerial perspective, warm-cool contrast, and alpine rock drawing.

### Graphics Engines, Point Clouds, and Radiance Fields

10. **Kerbl, B., Kopanas, G., Leimkühler, T., & Drettakis, G. (2023).** *3D Gaussian Splatting for Real-Time Radiance Field Rendering.* ACM Transactions on Graphics (TOG), 42(4), Article 139. DOI: [10.1145/3592433](https://doi.org/10.1145/3592433).
    * Seminal paper introducing 3D Gaussian Splatting, deriving the anisotropic 3D-to-2D covariance projection matrix and GPU tile-based alpha-blending rasterizer.

11. **Huang, B., Yu, Z., Chen, A., Geiger, A., & Gao, S. (2024).** *2D Gaussian Splatting for Geometrically Accurate Radiance Fields.* ACM SIGGRAPH 2024 Conference Papers, Article 18. DOI: [10.1145/3641519.3657428](https://doi.org/10.1145/3641519.3657428).
    * Extends Gaussian splatting to 2D surfels, enforcing geometric ray-splat consistency and enabling rigorous surface mesh and DEM extraction.

12. **Boucheny, C. (2009).** *Interactive Visualization of Large Unstructured Point Clouds with Eye-Dome Lighting.* In *Proceedings of the IEEE Symposium on Interactive Ray Tracing*, Salt Lake City, UT.
    * First formalization of Eye-Dome Lighting (EDL), proving that screen-space depth-buffer shading generates high-contrast 3D relief on unlit point clouds without normal vectors.
