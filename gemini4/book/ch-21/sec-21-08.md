# Chapter 21: Bare-Earth Extraction (DSM to DTM), Data Gaps, Shadows, and Overhangs

*How objects are removed from a DSM to create a DTM, the hidden interpolation uncertainties under removed objects, and how to handle voids and vertical/overhanging geometry.*

## 21.8 Curated Key References

The seminal benchmark papers, algorithm specifications, and planetary data publications listed below establish the mathematical, physical, and computational foundations for bare-earth extraction, void inpainting, and 3D surface reconstruction.

---

### Foundational and Benchmark Literature

* **Axelsson, P.** (2000). DEM generation from airborne laser scanner data using triangulated irregular networks. *International Archives of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing*, 33(B4/1; PART 4), 110–117.  
  *The milestone paper introducing Progressive TIN Densification (PTD), deriving the facet-angle and distance thresholding criteria utilized in commercial LiDAR production.*

* **Hawker, L., Uhe, P., Paulo, L., Sosa, J., Savage, J., Sampson, C., & Neal, J.** (2022). A 30 m global map of elevation with forests and buildings removed (FABDEM). *Environmental Research Letters*, 17(2), 024016. DOI: [10.1088/1748-9326/ac4d4f](https://doi.org/10.1088/1748-9326/ac4d4f)  
  *The landmark publication detailing the machine learning framework used to remove forest and building height biases from the global Copernicus GLO-30 DSM using GEDI and ICESat-2 calibration.*

* **Kazhdan, M., & Hoppe, H.** (2013). Screened Poisson surface reconstruction. *ACM Transactions on Graphics (TOG)*, 32(3), 29. DOI: [10.1145/2487228.2487237](https://doi.org/10.1145/2487228.2487237)  
  *Derives the screened Poisson partial differential equation for extracting watertight, continuous 3D triangular meshes from unorganized oriented point clouds with complex overhangs.*

* **Pingel, T. J., Clarke, K. C., & McBride, W. A.** (2013). An improved simple morphological filter for the terrain classification of airborne LIDAR data. *ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing*, 77, 21–30. DOI: [10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2012.12.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2012.12.002)  
  *Introduces the Simple Morphological Filter (SMRF), optimizing progressive erosion/dilation iterations and slope-dependent threshold scaling.*

* **Sithole, G., & Vosselman, G.** (2004). Experimental comparison of filter algorithms for bare-Earth extraction from airborne laser scanning point clouds. *ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing*, 59(1–2), 85–101. DOI: [10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2004.05.004](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2004.05.004)  
  *The definitive international benchmark defining Type I (omission) and Type II (commission) ground filtering errors and comparing performance across urban and rural topographies.*

* **Zhang, K., Chen, S. C., Whitman, D., Shyu, M. L., Yan, J., & Zhang, C.** (2003). A progressive morphological filter for removing non-ground measurements from airborne LIDAR data. *IEEE Transactions on Geoscience and Remote Sensing*, 41(4), 872–882. DOI: [10.1109/TGRS.2003.810682](https://doi.org/10.1109/TGRS.2003.810682)  
  *The original paper deriving the mathematical formulation of Progressive Morphological Filtering (PMF) with dynamically expanding structuring elements.*

* **Zhang, W., Qi, J., Wan, P., Wang, H., Xie, D., Wang, X., & Yan, G.** (2016). An easy-to-use airborne LiDAR data filtering method based on cloth simulation. *Remote Sensing*, 8(6), 538. DOI: [10.3390/rs8060538](https://doi.org/10.3390/rs8060538)  
  *Derives the Cloth Simulation Filter (CSF), formulating bare-earth extraction as physical cloth draping over inverted point clouds.*
