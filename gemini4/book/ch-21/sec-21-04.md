### 21.4.1 Exact Computational Geometry and the Airborne LiDAR Boom (1996–2000)

#### 1996: `CGAL` and Volumetric Signed Distance Reconstruction
Two foundational computational geometry milestones arrived in **1996**:
* **The Computational Geometry Algorithms Library (`CGAL`, 1996 in `gis-history`):** Founded by a European research consortium (INRIA Sophia Antipolis, MPI Informatik, Utrecht University, ETH Zürich, Tel Aviv University, FU Berlin), `CGAL` solved the numerical instability that caused early TIN-based ground filters to crash or loop infinitely when collinear scan-line points triggered IEEE-754 floating-point `InCircle` sign flips (Chapter 17). By providing exact-predicate, filtered-kernel 2D/3D Delaunay triangulations, alpha shapes, and surface mesh data structures, `CGAL` became the geometric backbone of modern open-source terrain processing.
* **Curless & Levoy's Volumetric Range Integration (`SIGGRAPH 1996`):** Working on Stanford's Digital Michelangelo / Cyberware laser scans, **Brian Curless and Marc Levoy (1996)** introduced the weighted **Truncated Signed Distance Field (TSDF)**—demonstrating how noisy multi-view 3D range scans with overhangs, self-occlusions, and unmeasured holes could be fused incrementally into watertight meshes with explicit free-space carving.

#### 2000: Peter Axelsson's Progressive TIN Densification (`PTD`)
As commercial airborne laser scanning (ALS) expanded in the late 1990s, early raster kernel filters struggled violently wherever flat urban floodplains met steep mountain scarps. At the **2000** XIX ISPRS Congress in Amsterdam, Swedish researcher **Peter Axelsson (2000)** presented *DEM Generation from Airborne Laser Scanner Data Using Triangulated Irregular Networks*. By seeding a coarse Delaunay TIN from block-minimum points and iteratively inserting returns governed by local facet-plane angle and distance thresholds, Axelsson allowed the reference surface itself to conform to arbitrary 3D terrain slopes. Integrated by Arttu Soininen into **Terrasolid `TerraScan`**, `PTD` became the industrial workhorse behind national LiDAR bare-earth mapping programs across Europe and North America.

### 21.4.2 The 2000 SRTM Void Crisis and the ISPRS Filter Benchmark (2000–2008)

#### February 2000: Space Shuttle *Endeavour* (`SRTM`) and $800\,000\text{ km}^2$ of Missing Earth
During an 11-day mission in **February 2000** (`gis-history`), Space Shuttle *Endeavour* (STS-99) deployed a $60\text{ m}$ outboard mast to acquire single-pass C-band and X-band bistatic InSAR between $60^\circ\text{N}$ and $56^\circ\text{S}$. When unedited SRTM tiles were released beginning in 2003, users discovered a sobering physical reality: **over $800\,000\text{ km}^2$ of land surface consisted of `NoData = -32768` voids**!
* Steep alpine cordilleras (the Himalayas, Karakoram, Andes, Alps, and Alaska / Coast Mountains) suffered severe **radar shadow** and **layover** phase-unwrapping failures; hyper-arid dune fields in the Sahara, Rub' al Khali, and Namib suffered low microwave backscatter SNR; and river gorges and lakes suffered water specular reflection.
* Early naive bilinear or pure-spline fills across $10\text{ km}$-wide Himalayan voids truncated $7000\text{ m}$ peaks by over $800\text{ m}$ or created grotesque overshoot craters.

This planetary void crisis catalyzed a decade of rigorous inpainting research:
1. **The Delta Surface Fill (`DSF`) Method (`Grohman, Kroenung, & Strebeck, 2006`):** Rather than pasting an auxiliary DEM (such as GTOPO30 or digitized topographic contours) directly into an SRTM hole—which creates $20\text{–}100\text{ m}$ vertical cliff seams around the void perimeter due to datum and resolution differences—Grohman et al. computed the difference ("delta") surface $\Delta z = z_{\text{SRTM}} - z_{\text{aux}}$ on valid pixels surrounding the void, interpolated the *smooth low-frequency delta surface* across the interior hole, and added it back to the high-frequency auxiliary DEM ($z_{\text{filled}} = z_{\text{aux}} + \widehat{\Delta z}$)—a direct precursor to modern Remove-Compute-Restore and Poisson composite blending (Chapter 23)!
2. **Multi-Scale Void-Filling Benchmark (`Reuter, Nelson, & Jarvis, 2007`):** Powering the widely adopted CGIAR-CSI SRTM v4 archive, Reuter et al. systematically proved that no single interpolator is optimal across void scales: **Kriging / IDW** excels on tiny pinhole voids ($<5\text{–}15\text{ pixels}$), **spline / ANUDEM** excels on medium valley/ridge voids when aided by contour topology, and **auxiliary Delta Surface Fill** is mandatory once void width exceeds the terrain correlation scale (culminating in **NASADEM [2020]**, which re-unwrapped raw SRTM interferograms using ICESat GLAS elevation control and ASTER/AW3D30 void fusion).

#### 2003–2007: `PMF`, the ISPRS Sithole-Vosselman Benchmark, and `MCC`
Concurrently in airborne LiDAR research:
* **Zhang et al. (2003)** formulated the **Progressive Morphological Filter (`PMF`)**, introducing slope-scaled dynamic height thresholds across expanding erosion-dilation windows.
* **Sithole and Vosselman (2004)** published the landmark **ISPRS Filter Test**, distributing 15 standardized reference sites (`CSite1`–`CSite8`) and establishing the universal Type I / Type II quantitative evaluation protocol still used to benchmark every new ML and geometric ground filter today.
* **Evans and Hudak (2007)** introduced **Multiscale Curvature Classification (`MCC`)** for dense Pacific Northwest conifer canopies, followed by **Bartels and Wei (2010)** formalizing threshold-free **Skewness Balancing**.

### 21.4.3 Open-Source Democratization, 3D Cliff Meshing, and Global Bare Earth (2011–Present)

#### 2011–2016: `PDAL`, `PoissonRecon` Screening, `SMRF`, and `CSF`
In **2011** (`gis-history`), Howard Butler and collaborators launched the open-source **Point Data Abstraction Library (`PDAL`)**, bringing reproducible, cloud-scalable C++/JSON pipelines to point-cloud processing. Within five years, three major open-source algorithmic advances converged inside `PDAL`, `WhiteboxTools`, and `CloudCompare`:
* **Kazhdan and Hoppe (2013)** added explicit positional point screening to 2006 Poisson Surface Reconstruction (`PoissonRecon`), enabling millimeter-faithful 3D watertight meshing of overhanging coastal cliffs, sea caves, and karst arches.
* **Pingel, Clarke, and McBride (2013)** published **`SMRF`** (`PDAL filters.smrf`), pairing linear one-pixel window progression with iterative thin-plate spline object inpainting.
* **Wuming Zhang et al. (2016)** published the **Cloth Simulation Filter (`CSF`, `PDAL filters.csf`)**, providing an intuitive physics-based ground extractor requiring minimal parameter tuning across flat cities and rugged mountains alike.

#### 2018–2022: Spaceborne Laser Calibration (`ICESat-2` & `GEDI`) Unlocks `FABDEM`
The launch of **ICESat-2** (`2018-09` in `gis-history`, $532\text{ nm}$ photon-counting ATLAS) and **ISS GEDI** (`2018-12` in `gis-history`, $1064\text{ nm}$ full-waveform sub-canopy ground ranging) finally supplied tens of billions of globally distributed sub-canopy bare-earth elevation control points across tropical rainforests and dense cities. Leveraging this spaceborne laser ground truth, **Laurence Hawker et al. (2022)** released **FABDEM** (*Forest And Buildings removed Copernicus DEM*), reducing global forest median vertical bias in Copernicus GLO-30 from $+5\text{–}18\text{ m}$ down to near-zero and inaugurating a new generation of machine-learning global bare-earth terrain models (including DiluviumDEM and FathomDEM).

### 21.4.4 Parallel Evolution in Hydrographic Seabed Filtering and Holiday Standards (2000–Present)

Across the exact same decades, hydrography confronted an analogous twin challenge—separating the true mineral seabed from biological/structural water-column clutter while governing unmapped seafloor "holidays":

* **2000–2006 (`NOAA HSSD`, `CUBE`, and `BAG`):** Beginning with the first **NOAA Hydrographic Surveys Specifications and Deliverables (`HSSD`, 2000 in `gis-history`)** and **Brian Calder's (2003) `CUBE` (Combined Uncertainty and Bathymetry Estimator)**, hydrographic offices replaced manual sounding-by-sounding editing with sequential Bayesian Kalman hypothesis tracking. Rather than blindly taking a minimum or morphological opening (which would either lock onto fish schools and kelp canopies or shave off shipwreck masts!), `CUBE` tracks multiple competing depth hypotheses per grid node and selects the seabed surface via local hypothesis disambiguation, storing both elevation and uncertainty inside **2006 Open Navigation Surface `BAG`** files while preserving verified hazards on an explicit override `Tracking List`.
* **2019–2022 (`IHO S-44` 6th Edition Bathymetric Coverage Rules):** Whereas terrestrial mapping historically tolerated unflagged interpolation across forest canopy voids, **IHO S-44 (6th ed., 2020)** formalized strict quantitative **Bathymetric Coverage** mandates: **Exclusive Order** and **Special Order** require **$100\%$ bathymetric coverage** (and $200\%$ in Exclusive Order high-risk channels) with strict cube-feature detection limits ($0.5\text{ m}$ to $1.0\text{ m}$), explicitly prohibiting unflagged mathematical interpolation across unensonified inter-swath holidays or acoustic shadow zones behind wrecks and reefs.
* **2019–2024 (3D Point-Cloud Deep Learning & `COPC`):** Following `PointNet++` (2017), **`KPConv` (Thomas et al., 2019)**, **`RandLA-Net` (Hu et al., 2020)**, and the **2021 Cloud-Optimized Point Cloud (`COPC`)** octree standard (`gis-history`), national mapping agencies (IGN France *LiDAR HD*, Swisstopo, USGS 3DEP) began transitioning production pipelines from purely geometric TIN/morphological rules to hybrid deep-learning + physical TIN workflows that simultaneously classify ground, bridge decks, vegetation strata, and coastal bathymetry directly on streaming cloud-hosted archives.
