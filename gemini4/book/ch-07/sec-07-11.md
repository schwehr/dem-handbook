## 7.11 Curated Key References

The literature on surveying platforms spans terrestrial geodesy, automotive robotics, aerospace engineering, naval architecture, satellite astrodynamics, and coastal physical oceanography. Rather than treating a platform merely as a passive vehicle, the foundational works curated below demonstrate how platform kinematics, trajectory observability, viewing geometry, and hydrodynamic or aerodynamic stability directly govern the Total Horizontal Uncertainty ($\text{THU}$), Total Vertical Uncertainty ($\text{TVU}$), spatial frequency of systematic artifacts, and occlusion topology of the resulting Digital Elevation Model (DEM). The references are organized into five thematic domains matching the taxonomy of Chapter 7, followed by a practitioner diagnostic matrix linking platform-induced error signatures to their mathematical remedies and open-source software implementations.

### 7.11.1 Cross-Platform Synthesis, Pedestrian Surveying, and Mobile Mapping Systems (MMS)

1. **Toth, C., & Jóźków, G. (2016).** Remote sensing platforms and sensors: A survey. *ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing*, 115, 22–36. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2015.10.004
   * **Platform & Kinematic Focus:** Establishes the modern taxonomy of remote sensing platforms across spaceborne, crewed airborne, UAS, terrestrial mobile, marine, and pedestrian regimes, formalizing trade-offs between altitude, ground speed, endurance, payload mass/power budgets, and direct georeferencing (GNSS/INS) requirements.
   * **DEM Uncertainty & Error Budget Implications:** Demonstrates how platform vibration spectra and dynamic trajectory uncertainty couple into sensor line-of-sight vectors, dictating the minimum achievable ground sampling distance (GSD), boresight stability, and high-frequency surface roughness noise.
   * **Validation & Operational Guidance:** Provides comparative performance envelopes across platform classes to guide survey mission design before selecting specific optical, radar, or LiDAR payloads.

2. **Puente, I., González-Jorge, H., Martínez-Sánchez, J., & Arias, P. (2013).** Review of mobile mapping and surveying technologies. *Measurement*, 46(7), 2127–2145. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.measurement.2013.03.006
   * **Platform & Kinematic Focus:** Dissects the hardware architecture of automotive and rail-based Mobile Mapping Systems (MMS), comparing tactical-grade and navigation-grade IMUs, dual-frequency GNSS receivers, wheel-mounted Distance Measurement Indicators (DMIs), and multi-head LiDAR profilers.

   * **Platform & Kinematic Focus:** Evaluates a modular, lightweight kinematic laser scanning payload deployed across pedestrian backpacks, automobiles, tethered balloons, and light aircraft while holding the core LiDAR/GNSS/INS sensor package constant.
   * **DEM Uncertainty & Error Budget Implications:** Isolates platform-specific kinematic error signatures—such as $1\text{–}2\text{ Hz}$ human gait pitch/roll oscillations on backpack mounts versus high-frequency aerodynamic buffeting on aircraft—providing an empirical benchmark for separating intrinsic rangefinder variance from platform trajectory errors.
   * **Validation & Operational Guidance:** Details planar patch calibration routines for solving 3D lever-arm and boresight parameters across rapidly reconfigured mobile mounts.

4. **Zhang, J., & Singh, S. (2014).** LOAM: Lidar Odometry and Mapping in real-time. *Proceedings of Robotics: Science and Systems (RSS X)*, Berkeley, CA, pp. 1–9. https://doi.org/10.15607/RSS.2014.X.007
   * **Platform & Kinematic Focus:** Introduces the foundational real-time LiDAR Simultaneous Localization and Mapping (SLAM) algorithm that separates high-frequency ($10\text{ Hz}$) scan-to-scan motion estimation (extracting sharp edge and planar surface features) from lower-frequency ($1\text{ Hz}$) scan-to-map registration.
   * **DEM Uncertainty & Error Budget Implications:** Enables continuous 3D point-cloud and DEM generation on handheld, backpack, cave, and forest-canopy platforms operating in complete GNSS denial, while demonstrating why loop-closure constraints or local control points are essential to bound cumulative vertical drift over trajectories exceeding $100\text{ m}$.
   * **Validation & Operational Guidance:** Forms the algorithmic core of modern tightly coupled LiDAR-inertial odometry frameworks (`LIO-SAM`, `FAST-LIO2`) used for pedestrian and subterranean terrain mapping.

5. **Luetzenburg, G., Kroon, A., & Bjørk, A. A. (2021).** Evaluation of the Apple iPhone 12 Pro LiDAR for an application in geosciences. *Scientific Reports*, 11(1), 22221. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-021-01763-9

   * **DEM Uncertainty & Error Budget Implications:** Quantifies the $5\text{ m}$ effective range limit, spatial resolution smoothing on centimeter-scale micro-topography, and VIO trajectory drift over tens of meters on coastal cliffs, establishing accuracy bounds ($\pm 1\text{–}10\text{ cm}$ local) for crowd-sourced pedestrian elevation capture.
   * **Validation & Operational Guidance:** Demonstrates practical field validation protocols using `CloudCompare` (`M3C2` point-cloud differencing) and surveyed ground control targets to tie opportunistic smartphone scans into geodetic reference frames.

### 7.11.2 Airborne Platforms: Balloons, Drones (UAS), and Crewed Aircraft

   * **DEM Uncertainty & Error Budget Implications:** Proves that uniform flight altitude and parallel viewing rays induce a projective correlation between interior radial lens distortion ($k_1$) and exterior camera altitude ($Z_0$), causing systematic quadratic "doming" or "bowling" vertical deformation ($\Delta z \propto r^2$) that is eliminated by adding $15^\circ\text{–}25^\circ$ convergent oblique flight lines.
   * **Validation & Operational Guidance:** Establishes flight-planning rules implemented across modern mission planners (`ArduPilot`, `PX4`, `UgCS`) and open-source photogrammetry suites (`OpenDroneMap`, `MicMac`).

   * **DEM Uncertainty & Error Budget Implications:** Demonstrates that flying a rolling-shutter drone at moderate-to-high speeds ($v > 5\text{ m s}^{-1}$) without modeling intra-frame camera motion shears image geometry and introduces systematic elevation biases of $2\text{–}5\times\text{GSD}$; formulates time-dependent collinearity equations that restore global-shutter-equivalent DEM accuracy.

   * **DEM Uncertainty & Error Budget Implications:** Quantifies how aircraft altitude and scanner nadir angle ($15^\circ\text{–}20^\circ$ constant-incidence cone) govern eye-safety, green-pulse water-column penetration ($2\text{–}3$ Secchi depths), sea-surface wave-slope refraction corrections, and seamless ellipsoid-to-tidal vertical datum transformations across the coastal zone.
   * **Validation & Operational Guidance:** Defines the multi-sensor airborne coastal mapping paradigm adopted by NOAA National Geodetic Survey (NGS) and USACE national coastal mapping programs.

### 7.11.3 Marine Platforms: Survey Vessels, USVs, Towed Bodies, ROVs, and AUVs

10. **Barrass, C. B. (2004).** *Ship Design and Performance for Masters and Mates*. Oxford, UK: Elsevier / Butterworth-Heinemann, 272 pp. https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-7506-6000-6.X5000-4
    * **Platform & Kinematic Focus:** Provides the definitive naval architectural formulation for vessel hydrodynamic settlement, dynamic trim, and squat as a function of hull block coefficient ($C_B$), speed through water ($v$), and channel blockage factor / depth Froude number ($\text{Fr}_h = v / \sqrt{gh}$), governing the instantaneous waterline draft $z_{\text{waterline}}(v, t) = z_{\text{static draft}} - \Delta z_{\text{fuel/ballast}}(t) + C_B \frac{v^2}{g} f(\text{Fr}_h)$.
    * **DEM Uncertainty & Error Budget Implications:** Because dynamic squat quadratically depresses a survey vessel's hull and transducer closer to the seabed ($\Delta z_{\text{squat}} \propto C_B v^2$, intensifying up to twofold in shallow, confined waterways where $h/T < 1.5$), uncompensated squat shortens measured acoustic ranges and introduces a speed-dependent **shoal depth bias** ($\Delta \hat{h} = -\Delta z_{\text{squat}}$, or positive seabed elevation bias of $10\text{–}40\text{ cm}$) into non-ERS hydrographic DEMs, whereas uncompensated fuel burn ($\Delta z_{\text{fuel}} > 0$) raises the hull and introduces a dangerous **deep depth bias**.
    * **Validation & Operational Guidance:** Forms the physical basis for empirical vessel squat curves (speed-step trials against PPK/RTK GNSS) and daily fuel/ballast draft logs required under IHO S-44 and NOAA HSSD hydrographic surveys when Ellipsoidally Referenced Surveying (ERS) is unavailable.

11. **Hughes Clarke, J. E. (2003).** Dynamic motion residuals in swath sonar data: Ironing out the creases. *International Hydrographic Review*, 4(1), 6–23.
    * **Platform & Kinematic Focus:** Performs a forensic decomposition of dynamic vessel motion (roll, pitch, heave, yaw) and sensor time synchronization on crewed survey launches and Uncrewed Surface Vessels (USVs) operating in ocean swell.
    * **DEM Uncertainty & Error Budget Implications:** Derives the exact cross-track and along-track geometric signatures of roll boresight ($\delta z = -R \sin\theta\,\delta\phi = -y\,\delta\phi = -h\tan\theta\,\delta\phi$), pitch, heave, and latency residuals—explaining how outer-swath motion "washboard" ribs are separated from sound-velocity profile (SVP) refraction "smiles/frowns" via the hydrographic patch test.
    * **Validation & Operational Guidance:** Provides the standard diagnostic decision tree implemented in `MB-System` (`mbpatchtest`) and commercial hydrographic processing suites (`Qimera`, `CARIS HIPS`).

12. **Wynn, R. B., Huvenne, V. A. I., Le Bas, T. P., Murton, B. J., Connelly, D. P., Bett, B. J., Ruhl, H. A., Morris, K. J., Peakall, J., Parsons, D. R., Sumner, E. J., Darby, S. E., Dorrell, R. M., & Hunt, J. E. (2014).** Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs): Their past, present and future contributions to the advancement of marine geoscience. *Marine Geology*, 352, 451–468. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.margeo.2014.03.012
    * **Platform & Kinematic Focus:** Synthesizes the deployment of deep-submergence Autonomous Underwater Vehicles (AUVs) flying terrain-following trajectories $5\text{–}50\text{ m}$ above the seabed at water depths down to $6000\text{ m}$.
    * **DEM Uncertainty & Error Budget Implications:** Shows that decoupling the multibeam sonar from the sea surface shrinks the acoustic footprint ($D \approx 2 R \tan(\psi/2)$) from $\sim 70\text{ m}$ (surface hull at $4000\text{ m}$) to $< 0.5\text{ m}$ while eliminating surface wave motion and upper-ocean thermocline refraction, shifting the dominant DEM error source from vertical sounding noise to horizontal dead-reckoning drift.
    * **Validation & Operational Guidance:** Contrasts hull-mounted, deep-towed, ROV, and AUV resolution-versus-coverage trade-offs across submarine canyons, mid-ocean ridges, and benthic habitats.

13. **Paull, L., Saeedi, S., Seto, M., & Li, H. (2014).** AUV navigation and localization: A review. *IEEE Journal of Oceanic Engineering*, 39(1), 131–149. https://doi.org/10.1109/JOE.2013.2278891
    * **Platform & Kinematic Focus:** Reviews acoustic and inertial underwater positioning architectures—Ultra-Short Baseline (USBL), Long Baseline (LBL), Doppler Velocity Log (DVL) bottom-lock aiding, and Bathymetric SLAM—for submerged AUVs, ROVs, and deep-tow sleds deprived of GNSS signals.
    * **DEM Uncertainty & Error Budget Implications:** Quantifies unbounded horizontal uncertainty growth ($\text{THU}$ drifting at $0.05\%\text{–}0.2\%$ of distance traveled under DVL-INS dead reckoning) and demonstrates how cross-line terrain contour matching (`mbnavadjust` / Bathy-SLAM) eliminates duplicate ghosting of steep abyssal fault scarps.
    * **Validation & Operational Guidance:** Guides the selection of acoustic aiding (shipboard USBL vs. seabed LBL transponder arrays) and reciprocal cross-line spacing to bound subsea trajectory covariance.

14. **International Hydrographic Organization (IHO). (2020).** *IHO Standards for Hydrographic Surveys (S-44)*, Edition 6.0.0. Monaco: International Hydrographic Organization (see also Edition 6.1.0, 2022, and NOAA *Hydrographic Surveys Specifications and Deliverables* [HSSD]). https://iho.int/uploads/user/pubs/standards/s-44/S-44_Edition_6.0.0_EN.pdf
    * **Platform & Kinematic Focus:** Establishes the international regulatory framework for hydrographic survey platforms (crewed ships, USVs, AUVs, airborne bathymetric LiDAR), defining Exclusive Order, Special Order, and Orders 1a, 1b, and 2.
    * **DEM Uncertainty & Error Budget Implications:** Formalizes the $95\%$ confidence Total Horizontal Uncertainty ($\text{THU}$) and Total Vertical Uncertainty ($\text{TVU}_{\max} = \sqrt{a^2 + (b \times d)^2}$) limits—specifically Exclusive Order ($a = 0.15\text{ m}, b = 0.0075, \text{THU} = 1.0\text{ m}$), Special Order ($a = 0.25\text{ m}, b = 0.0075, \text{THU} = 2.0\text{ m}$), Order 1a/1b ($a = 0.50\text{ m}, b = 0.013, \text{THU} = 5\text{ m} + 0.05d$), and Order 2 ($a = 1.0\text{ m}, b = 0.023, \text{THU} = 20\text{ m} + 0.10d$)—requiring explicit error-budget accounting for platform draft, dynamic settlement/squat, heave, and attitude uncertainty.
    * **Validation & Operational Guidance:** Mandates *a priori* and *a posteriori* Total Propagated Uncertainty (TPU) verification, cross-line check statistics, and Bathymetric Attributed Grid (`.bag` / IHO S-102) uncertainty surface deliverables.

### 7.11.7 Synthesis Guidance for Practitioners

> [!TIP]
> **Cross-Referencing Platform Kinematics with Sensor Physics (Chapters 8–12):**
> When diagnosing systematic artifacts in a composite DEM, always separate **platform-trajectory errors** (documented in the references above) from **sensor-physics errors** (covered in Chapters 8–12). A periodic cross-track stripe in a satellite stereo DEM (Shean et al., 2016) or a multibeam swath (Hughes Clarke, 2003) is diagnostic of platform attitude oscillation ($R \sin\theta\,\delta\phi$), whereas a symmetric cross-swath "smile" or radial "dome" is diagnostic of unmodeled optical/acoustic refraction ($k_1$ radial lens distortion or water-column sound-velocity profile $c(z)$).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Archival Platform Metadata Preservation:**
> Because platform-induced biases (such as vessel speed squat, UAS rolling-shutter readout time, or satellite pushbroom attitude jitter) are functions of the instantaneous vehicle state vector $(\mathbf{r}(t), \mathbf{v}(t), \mathbf{R}(t))$, a gridded raster DEM alone cannot be retroactively corrected if those state vectors are discarded. Practitioners should archive raw trajectory files (`SBET` / `POS`, `GNSS` RINEX, vessel speed/draft logs, and satellite RPC/ephemeris metadata) alongside final deliverable DEMs to enable future reprocessing as trajectory and sensor models advance.
