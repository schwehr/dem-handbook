# Chapter 31: Planetary and Extraterrestrial DEMs (Moon, Mars, Asteroids, and Ocean Worlds)

*How mapping worlds without oceans, without GNSS, and sometimes without spherical symmetry forged modern photogrammetry and laser altimetry.*

---

## 31.8 Curated Key References

Planetary geodesy, extraterrestrial stereophotogrammetry, and small-body shape modeling represent the outer frontier of digital elevation science. The references below provide the authoritative peer-reviewed foundations for planetary reference datums, orbit crossover adjustments, spacecraft jitter correction, and polyhedral Roche potentials.

---

### Planetary Geodesy, Altimetry, and Coordinate Standards

1. **Smith, D. E., Zuber, M. T., Frey, H. V., Garvin, J. B., Head, J. W., Muhleman, D. O., ... & Zwally, H. J. (2001).** *Mars Orbiter Laser Altimeter: Experiment summary after the first year of global mapping of Mars.* Journal of Geophysical Research: Planets, 106(E10), 23689–23722. DOI: [10.1029/2000JE001364](https://doi.org/10.1029/2000JE001364).
   * Definitive overview of MOLA geodesy, establishing the Martian Areoid vertical datum ($W_0 = 12,680,000\text{ m}^2/\text{s}^2$), orbit crossover adjustment, and global topographic cartography.

2. **Archinal, B. A., Acton, C. H., A'Hearn, M. F., Conrad, A., Consolmagno, G. J., Duxbury, T., ... & Williams, I. P. (2018).** *Report of the IAU Working Group on Cartographic Coordinates and Rotational Elements: 2015.* Celestial Mechanics and Dynamical Astronomy, 130(3), 22. DOI: [10.1007/s10569-017-9805-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10569-017-9805-5).
   * The authoritative international standard codifying planetocentric vs. planetographic coordinate frames, triaxial reference ellipsoids, spin poles, and prime meridians across all solar system bodies.

3. **Acton, C. H. (1996).** *Ancillary data services of NASA's Navigation and Ancillary Information Facility.* Planetary and Space Science, 44(1), 65–70. DOI: [10.1016/0032-0633(95)00107-7](https://doi.org/10.1016/0032-0633(95)00107-7).
   * Primary citation for the NASA/JPL NAIF SPICE system (`SPK`, `PCK`, `CK`, `IK`, `FK`), detailing the observation geometry architecture used across all NASA deep-space missions.

4. **Mazarico, E., Rowlands, D. D., Neumann, G. A., Smith, D. E., Torrence, M. H., Lemoine, F. G., & Zuber, M. T. (2012).** *Orbit determination of the Lunar Reconnaissance Orbiter.* Journal of Geodesy, 86(3), 193–207. DOI: [10.1007/s00190-011-0509-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00190-011-0509-4).
   * Documents the multi-million-point laser altimeter crossover inversion (LOLA) that eliminated orbital ephemeris errors to construct the global geodetic grid of the Moon.

---

### Photogrammetry, Jitter, and Photoclinometry

5. **Beyer, R. A., Alexandrov, O., & McMichael, S. (2018).** *The Ames Stereo Pipeline: NASA's open source software for deriving and processing terrain data.* Earth and Space Science, 5(9), 537–548. DOI: [10.1029/2018EA000409](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018EA000409).
   * Architectural reference for the NASA Ames Stereo Pipeline (ASP), detailing automated line-scan stereo correlation, bundle adjustment, and sub-pixel elevation reconstruction.

6. **Alexandrov, O., & Beyer, R. A. (2018).** *Multiview shape-from-shading for planetary images.* Earth and Space Science, 5(10), 652–666. DOI: [10.1029/2018EA000390](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018EA000390).
   * Formulates multi-view photoclinometry constrained by laser altimetry, demonstrating sub-meter 3D reconstruction inside permanently shadowed lunar polar craters.

---

### Small Bodies, Polyhedral Potentials, and Cryogenic Seas

7. **Werner, R. A., & Scheeres, D. J. (1996).** *Exterior gravitation of a polyhedron derived and compared with harmonic and mascon gravitation representations of asteroid 4769 Castalia.* Celestial Mechanics and Dynamical Astronomy, 65(3), 313–344. DOI: [10.1007/BF00053511](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00053511).
   * Seminal mathematical paper deriving the exact closed-form analytic gravitational potential and acceleration of arbitrary 3D polyhedral triangular meshes.

8. **Gaskell, R. W., Barnouin-Jha, O. S., Scheeres, D. J., Konopliv, A. S., Mukai, T., Abe, S., ... & Yoshikawa, M. (2008).** *Characterizing and navigating small bodies with imaging data.* Meteoritics & Planetary Science, 43(6), 1049–1061. DOI: [10.1111/j.1945-5100.2008.tb00692.x](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1945-5100.2008.tb00692.x).
   * Details the Stereophotoclinometry (SPC) maplet methodology used by NASA and JAXA for asteroid shape modeling and spacecraft hazard avoidance.

9. **Mastrogiuseppe, M., Posiolova, L., Hayes, A. G., Seu, R., Lunine, J. I., & Le Gall, A. (2014).** *The bathymetry of a Titan sea.* Geophysical Research Letters, 41(5), 1432–1437. DOI: [10.1002/2013GL058618](https://doi.org/10.1002/2013GL058618).
   * Landmark discovery documenting Cassini RADAR altimetry penetration through liquid methane/ethane seas on Titan, measuring bathymetry down to $160\text{ m}$ depth.
