# Chapter 13: Survey Planning for Calibration, Error Reduction, and Monitoring

*How to design data acquisition in the field so systematic biases become mathematically observable, separable, and self-correcting.*

## 13.8 Curated Key References

The historical manuals, agency specifications, and peer-reviewed articles listed below define the operational methodologies and mathematical principles governing survey planning, boresight calibration, and quality assurance.

---

### Government Standards, Manuals, and Specifications

* **Hawley, J. H.** (1928). *Hydrographic Manual*. Special Publication No. 143. Washington, D.C.: U.S. Coast and Geodetic Survey, Department of Commerce.  
  *The landmark historical manual that first standardized orthogonal cross-lines, systematic sounding line spacing, and lead-line calibration protocols in the United States.*

* **International Hydrographic Organization (IHO).** (2020). *IHO Standards for Hydrographic Surveys*. Special Publication No. 44 (S-44), 6th Edition. Monaco: International Hydrographic Bureau.  
  *Defines global standards for hydrographic survey orders (Exclusive, Special, Order 1a/1b, Order 2), establishing mathematical Total Vertical Uncertainty (TVU) and Total Horizontal Uncertainty (THU) formulas.*

* **National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA).** (2020). *Field Procedures Manual (FPM) for Hydrographic Surveying*. Office of Coast Survey, National Ocean Service, Silver Spring, MD.  
  *The authoritative operational guide detailing multibeam patch-test maneuvers, moving vessel profiler (MVP) protocols, ellipsoidally referenced surveys (ERS), and real-time QA.*

* **National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration (NOAA).** (2024). *Hydrographic Surveys Specifications and Deliverables (HSSD)*. Office of Coast Survey, National Ocean Service, Silver Spring, MD.  
  *Binding technical specifications establishing mandatory 5% cross-line coverage, CUBE gridding parameters, and automated holiday detection requirements.*

* **Umbach, M. J.** (1976). *Hydrographic Manual, Fourth Edition*. Rockville, MD: National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration, National Ocean Survey.  
  *Seminal manual modernizing survey planning for the electronic era, formalizing the daily acoustic bar check and radio navigation geometric dilution constraints.*

* **U.S. Geological Survey (USGS).** (2022). *USGS National Geospatial Program Elevation Product Rating Guide and 3DEP Quality Levels*. USGS Techniques and Methods, book 11, chap. B11. Reston, VA: U.S. Geological Survey.  
  *Establishes pulse density requirements (QL1 >= 8 pulses/m^2, QL2 >= 2 pulses/m^2) and cross-line flight planning specifications for 3D Elevation Program (3DEP) LiDAR.*

### Seminal Papers and Methodological Literature

* **Calder, B. R., & Mayer, L. A.** (2003). Automated processing of high-rate, high-density multibeam echosounder data. *Geochemistry, Geophysics, Geosystems*, 4(6), 1048. DOI: [10.1029/2002GC000486](https://doi.org/10.1029/2002GC000486)  
  *The original paper deriving the Combined Uncertainty and Bathymetry Estimator (CUBE) multi-hypothesis Kalman filter algorithm.*

* **Godin, A.** (1998). *The Calibration of Shallow Water Multibeam Echo-Sounding Systems*. Master's thesis, Department of Geodesy and Geomatics Engineering, University of New Brunswick, Fredericton, NB, Canada.  
  *Derives the complete geometric formulation of the multibeam patch test, detailing parameter observability and residual sensitivity across variable seafloor slopes.*

* **Skaloud, J., & Lichti, D.** (2006). Rigorous approach to bore-sight self-calibration in airborne laser scanning. *ISPRS Journal of Photogrammetry and Remote Sensing*, 61(1), 47–59. DOI: [10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2006.07.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isprsjprs.2006.07.003)  
  *Rigorous derivation of the boresight observation equations, proving the rank deficiencies that occur over flat ground and defining optimal flight-strip geometries.*

* **van Rijssel, C. P., & van den Hoogen, H.** (2012). Quality control in hydrographic surveys: The role of cross-lines. *The International Hydrographic Review*, (8), 45–54.  
  *Empirical study evaluating cross-line junction statistics and demonstrating how orthogonal check-lines detect unmodeled vertical datum biases and sensor drift.*
