### 28.5.1 The Active Sonar Equation for Distributed Targets

For a continuous, distributed interface like the seafloor, acoustic scattering is distributed across an extended surface patch rather than concentrated in a discrete point reflector.

The acoustic power received at the transducer array (expressed as **Echo Level $\text{EL}$** in $\text{dB re } 1\,\mu\text{Pa}$) is governed by the active sonar equation:

$$\text{EL} = \text{SL} - 2\text{TL}(R) + \text{TS}_{\text{area}}$$
where:
* $\text{SL}$: Transmit Source Level ($\text{dB re } 1\,\mu\text{Pa at } 1\text{ meter}$).
* $\text{TL}(R)$: One-way transmission loss over slant range $R$ ($\text{dB}$).
* $\text{TS}_{\text{area}}$: Target Strength of the distributed ensonified area.

#### 1. Target Strength Decomposition
For a distributed surface, target strength is the product of the intrinsic **Bottom Backscattering Strength per unit area ($S_b$ or $BS$)** and the physical **ensonified footprint area ($A_{\text{footprint}}$)**:

$$\text{TS}_{\text{area}} = 10 \log_{10}\left( \int_{A_{\text{footprint}}} \sigma_b \, dA \right) \approx BS(\theta_g) + 10 \log_{10} A_{\text{footprint}}(\theta_g, R)$$
where $\sigma_b$ is the dimensionless differential scattering cross-section per unit solid angle per unit area, and $BS(\theta_g) = 10\log_{10}\sigma_b$ (expressed in $\text{dB re } 1\text{ m}^2/\text{m}^2$).

#### 2. Two-Way Transmission Loss
Accounting for spherical wave spreading and seawater absorption over two-way slant range $2R$:
$$2\text{TL}(R) = 40 \log_{10}(R) + 2 \cdot \alpha(f, T, S, P) \cdot R$$
where $\alpha$ is the absorption coefficient in $\text{dB/meter}$.

Substituting into the sonar equation yields:
$$\text{EL} = \text{SL} - \big( 40 \log_{10} R + 2 \alpha R \big) + BS(\theta_g) + 10 \log_{10} A_{\text{footprint}}(\theta_g, R)$$

### 28.5.2 Deriving the True Local Grazing Angle from the 3D DEM

Backscatter strength $BS(\theta_g)$ depends heavily on the angle between the incoming acoustic ray and the tangent plane of the seabed.

#### 1. Coordinate Definitions and Surface Normal
Let the seafloor elevation model be defined on a grid $z = f(x, y)$ in a Cartesian coordinate system where $+z$ points Upward.

The spatial surface gradients are:
$$p = \frac{\partial z}{\partial x}, \quad q = \frac{\partial z}{\partial y}$$

The outward-pointing unit normal vector derived directly from the DEM is:
$$\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM}} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{1 + p^2 + q^2}} \begin{pmatrix} -p \\ -q \\ 1 \end{pmatrix}$$

#### 2. Ray Vector and Grazing Angle Calculation
Let $\mathbf{x}_{\text{sensor}} = (x_s, y_s, z_s)^T$ be the physical 3D coordinate of the sonar transducer at ping transmission, and let $\mathbf{x}_{\text{seafloor}} = (x_b, y_b, z_b)^T$ be the sounding position on the seafloor.

The slant range vector is:
$$\mathbf{r} = \mathbf{x}_{\text{seafloor}} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{sensor}}, \quad R = \|\mathbf{r}\|$$
The incident ray unit vector pointing *toward* the seafloor is:
$$\hat{\mathbf{r}} = \frac{\mathbf{r}}{R}$$

The angle of incidence $\theta_i$ is the angle between the incoming ray and the inward surface normal ($-\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM}}$):
$$\cos\theta_i = -\hat{\mathbf{r}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM}}$$

The **local grazing angle $\theta_g$** is the complementary angle measured upward from the tangent plane to the ray:
$$\theta_g = 90^\circ - \theta_i \implies \sin\theta_g = \cos\theta_i = -\hat{\mathbf{r}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM}}$$

$$\theta_g = \arcsin\left( -\hat{\mathbf{r}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM}} \right)$$

*If the seafloor is assumed horizontal ($\hat{\mathbf{n}} = (0, 0, 1)^T$), then $\sin\theta_g = -r_z/R = \cos\phi_{\text{launch}}$. Any local slope angle $\beta$ shifts the true grazing angle by $\Delta \theta_g = \beta$, inducing errors exceeding $15\text{ dB}$ in backscatter strength if uncorrected!*

### 28.5.3 Ensonified Footprint Area ($A_{\text{footprint}}$)

The physical area of the seafloor contributing acoustic energy to a single time sample depends on whether the echo is bounded by the acoustic pulse duration or by the beamwidth aperture.

#### 1. Pulse-Length-Limited Regime (Oblique Angles)
At oblique angles ($\theta_g \ll 90^\circ$), the transmit pulse of duration $\tau$ ($seconds$) projects across the seabed. The instantaneous radial length ensonified on the slope is:
$$L_{\text{across}} = \frac{c \tau}{2 \cos\theta_g}$$
where $c$ is the sound speed at the seabed.

The along-track width of the footprint is governed by the two-way along-track beamwidth $\Delta \phi_{\text{along}}$ ($\text{radians}$):
$$L_{\text{along}} = R \cdot \Delta \phi_{\text{along}}$$

The ensonified area in the pulse-length-limited regime is:
$$A_{\text{pulse}}(\theta_g, R) = L_{\text{across}} \cdot L_{\text{along}} = \frac{c \tau R \Delta \phi_{\text{along}}}{2 \cos\theta_g}$$

#### 2. Beam-Aperture-Limited Regime (Near Nadir)
As the ray approaches normal incidence ($\theta_g \to 90^\circ$), $\cos\theta_g \to 0$, causing $A_{\text{pulse}}$ to diverge toward infinity. In reality, the footprint cannot exceed the geometric ellipse bounded by the beamwidth apertures:
$$L_{\text{across, beam}} = R \cdot \Delta \theta_{\text{across}}$$
$$L_{\text{along, beam}} = R \cdot \Delta \phi_{\text{along}}$$

The beam-limited footprint area projected onto the seabed tangent plane (inclined at incidence angle $\theta_i = 90^\circ - \theta_g$, where $\cos\theta_i = \sin\theta_g$) is an ellipse (Lurton & Lamarche, 2015):
$$A_{\text{beam}}(\theta_g, R) = \frac{\pi}{4\sin\theta_g} \big( R \cdot \Delta \theta_{\text{across}} \big) \big( R \cdot \Delta \phi_{\text{along}} \big)$$

#### 3. Continuous Effective Area Formulation
In robust processing algorithms (such as the Lurton & Lamarche 2015 formulation), the effective footprint area is evaluated as the harmonic combination:

$$A_{\text{footprint}}(\theta_g, R) = \frac{A_{\text{pulse}} \cdot A_{\text{beam}}}{\sqrt{A_{\text{pulse}}^2 + A_{\text{beam}}^2}} = \frac{\frac{c \tau R \Delta \phi}{2\cos\theta_g}}{\sqrt{1 + \left( \frac{2 c \tau \tan\theta_g}{\pi R \Delta \theta} \right)^2}}$$
This closed-form formulation transitions smoothly from the beam-limited specular disk at nadir to the pulse-limited strip at outer beams without numerical singularities.

### 28.5.4 Inverting for Calibrated Seafloor Backscattering Strength

Solving the active sonar equation for the true bottom backscattering strength $BS(\theta_g)$:

$$BS(\theta_g) = \text{EL} - \text{SL} + 40 \log_{10}(R) + 2 \alpha_{\text{ac}} R - 10 \log_{10} A_{\text{footprint}}(\theta_g, R)$$

By explicitly evaluating $R$, $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$, $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM}}$, and $\theta_g$ at every point on the seabed using the co-registered 3D DEM, all sensor transmission losses, footprint geometry distortions, and topographic slope shadings are eliminated. The resulting $BS(\theta_g)$ surface represents an intrinsic acoustic property of the seabed substrate, suitable for objective geoscientific and biological habitat classification.

### 28.5.5 Radiometric Normalization for LiDAR Intensity and SAR Terrain Flattening

The exact same 3D DEM surface normal $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM}} = [-p, -q, 1]^\top / \sqrt{1 + p^2 + q^2}$ governs radiometric calibration in optical laser scanning and microwave radar:

#### 1. LiDAR Pseudo-Reflectance Normalization (Höfle & Pfeifer, 2007)
For an extended Lambertian surface with recorded laser pulse intensity $I_{\text{raw}}$, slant range $R = \|\mathbf{x}_{\text{target}} - \mathbf{x}_{\text{sensor}}\|_2$, and true DEM incidence angle $\cos\theta_i = -\hat{\mathbf{r}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM}}$, the range- and angle-normalized intensity $I_{\text{norm}}$ referenced to standard altitude $R_{\text{ref}}$ and nadir incidence ($\theta_{i,\text{ref}} = 0^\circ$) is:
$$I_{\text{norm}} = I_{\text{raw}} \cdot \left(\frac{R}{R_{\text{ref}}}\right)^2 \cdot \frac{\exp\big(2 \alpha_{\text{atm}}(R - R_{\text{ref}})\big)}{\big(-\hat{\mathbf{r}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM}}\big)^k}$$
where $k = 1$ for an ideal Lambertian scatterer, or $k \in [0.6, 1.2]$ in generalized Minnaert / Lommel-Seeliger rough-terrain reflectance models.

#### 2. SAR Terrain-Flattened Gamma-Naught ($\gamma_T^0$, Small, 2011)
In Synthetic Aperture Radar, a single slant-range / azimuth pixel $(r, a)$ receives energy from a collection of 3D DEM surface facets $k \in \mathcal{F}(r, a)$ due to topographic foreshortening and layover. Let $A_{3\text{D},k} = \Delta x \Delta y \sqrt{1 + p_k^2 + q_k^2}$ denote the true 3D surface area of DEM facet $k$, and let $\theta_{i,k} = \arccos(-\hat{\mathbf{r}}_k \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM},k})$ be its local incidence angle relative to the radar look vector $\hat{\mathbf{r}}_k$. The local illuminated area projected into the plane perpendicular to the radar line of sight is $A_{\gamma,k} = A_{3\text{D},k} \cos\theta_{i,k}$. Integrating over all DEM facets contributing to radar bin $(r, a)$ yields the homomorphic terrain-flattened backscatter $\gamma_T^0$:
$$\gamma_T^0(r, a) = \frac{\beta^0(r, a) \, A_{\beta}}{\sum_{k \in \mathcal{F}(r,a)} A_{3\text{D},k} \max\big(0, -\hat{\mathbf{r}}_k \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM},k}\big)}$$
where $A_\beta = \Delta r_{\text{slant}} \Delta a_{\text{az}}$ is the nominal slant-range pixel area.

#### 3. Propagating DEM Slope Uncertainty into Backscatter Error ($\sigma_{BS}$)
Because DEM derivatives $(p, q)$ amplify high-frequency elevation noise $\sigma_z$ inversely with grid spacing ($\sigma_p = \sigma_q \approx \frac{\sigma_z}{\sqrt{2}\Delta x}$), uncertainty in the DEM surface normal propagates directly into grazing-angle uncertainty $\sigma_{\theta_g}$ (in radians):
$$\sigma_{\theta_g}^2 \approx \left(\frac{\partial \theta_g}{\partial p}\right)^2 \sigma_p^2 + \left(\frac{\partial \theta_g}{\partial q}\right)^2 \sigma_q^2 \approx \cos^4\alpha \, \sigma_p^2 = \sigma_\alpha^2 \quad (\text{look-aligned slope; bounded by } \cos^2\alpha\,\sigma_p^2)$$
In the pulse-length-limited regime ($A_{\text{pulse}} \propto 1/\cos\theta_g$), an error $\delta\theta_g$ (in radians) perturbs both the footprint area term ($-10\log_{10} A_{\text{pulse}}$) and the physical angular response curve $BS(\theta_g)$:
$$\delta BS_{\text{total}}(\theta_g) \approx \left( \frac{\partial BS}{\partial \theta_g} - \frac{10}{\ln 10} \tan\theta_g \right) \delta\theta_g$$
This reveals a fundamental scale trade-off: if the DEM used for slope correction is gridded too finely without smoothing noisy outer-beam soundings, derivative noise $\sigma_p$ injects speckle noise into the backscatter mosaic; if gridded too coarsely, real bedform slopes are smoothed out, leaving residual topographic shading.

### 28.5.6 Worked Numerical Calculation: 3D DEM Slope vs. Flat-Seafloor Backscatter Error

Consider a $300\text{ kHz}$ multibeam echosounder ($\alpha_{\text{ac}} = 0.070\text{ dB/m}$, sound speed $c = 1500\text{ m/s}$, pulse length $\tau = 0.20\text{ ms} = 2.0 \times 10^{-4}\text{ s}$, beamwidths $\Delta\theta_{\text{across}} = \Delta\phi_{\text{along}} = 1.0^\circ = 0.017453\text{ rad}$, Source Level $\text{SL} = 215.0\text{ dB re } 1\,\mu\text{Pa at } 1\text{ m}$) ensonifying a seafloor point at slant range $R = 50.0\text{ m}$ and across-track beam launch angle $\theta_{\text{launch}} = 50.0^\circ$ from nadir in the $+x$ (East) direction, so the incident ray unit vector is $\hat{\mathbf{r}} = [\sin 50^\circ, 0, -\cos 50^\circ]^\top = [0.7660, 0, -0.6428]^\top$. The recorded Echo Level is $\text{EL} = 118.0\text{ dB re } 1\,\mu\text{Pa}$.

Suppose the seafloor at this location is a sandbank sloping upward toward $+x$ with gradient $p = \partial z/\partial x = +0.3640 = +\tan 20.0^\circ$ (a $20.0^\circ$ upslope whose West-facing flank tilts back toward the sonar at $x = 0$) and $q = 0$:

1. **Flat-Seafloor Assumption ($\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{flat}} = [0, 0, 1]^\top$):**
   * Apparent grazing angle: $\sin\theta_{g,\text{flat}} = -\hat{\mathbf{r}}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{flat}} = 0.6428 \implies \theta_{g,\text{flat}} = 40.0^\circ$ ($\cos 40.0^\circ = 0.7660$).
   * Apparent pulse-limited area:
     $$A_{\text{pulse,flat}} = \frac{(1500)(2.0 \times 10^{-4})(50.0)(0.017453)}{2 (0.7660)} = \frac{0.2618}{1.5321} = 0.1709\text{ m}^2 \implies 10\log_{10} A_{\text{pulse,flat}} = -7.67\text{ dB re } 1\text{ m}^2$$
   * Apparent backscatter (with $40\log_{10}(50) = 67.96\text{ dB}$ and $2\alpha_{\text{ac}} R = 7.00\text{ dB}$):
     $$BS_{\text{flat}} = 118.0 - 215.0 + 67.96 + 7.00 - (-7.67) = -14.37\text{ dB}$$

2. **True 3D DEM Normal Correction ($\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM}}$):**
   * Unit normal: $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{\text{DEM}} = \frac{[-p, 0, 1]^\top}{\sqrt{1 + (+0.3640)^2}} = [-0.3420, 0, +0.9397]^\top$.
   * True local grazing angle:
     $$\sin\theta_{g,\text{true}} = -\big((+0.7660)(-0.3420) + (0)(0) + (-0.6428)(+0.9397)\big) = -\big(-0.2620 - 0.6040\big) = +0.8660 \implies \theta_{g,\text{true}} = 60.0^\circ$$
   * True pulse-limited area ($\cos 60.0^\circ = 0.5000$):
     $$A_{\text{pulse,true}} = \frac{0.2618}{2(0.5000)} = 0.2618\text{ m}^2 \implies 10\log_{10} A_{\text{pulse,true}} = -5.82\text{ dB re } 1\text{ m}^2$$
   * True footprint-corrected backscatter:
     $$BS_{\text{true}}(60.0^\circ) = 118.0 - 215.0 + 67.96 + 7.00 - (-5.82) = -16.22\text{ dB}$$
   * Furthermore, on medium sand, physical backscattering strength increases by $\sim +5.5\text{ dB}$ between $\theta_g = 40^\circ$ and $\theta_g = 60^\circ$. Without the 3D DEM normal, the processor both underestimates the footprint area by $1.85\text{ dB}$ *and* evaluates the ARA curve at the wrong angle ($40^\circ$ instead of $60^\circ$), producing a combined **$>7.3\text{ dB}$ radiometric bias** that misclassifies the sand slope as coarse gravel or rock!
