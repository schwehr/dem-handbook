# Chapter 16: Discrete Global Grid Systems (S2, H3) and Special Location Coding Schemes

*Indexing a spherical/ellipsoidal planet without polar singularities or projection seams—and the pitfalls of geocoding schemes.*

## 16.8 Curated Key References

The seminal mathematical papers, open standards specifications, and critical technical reports listed below establish the theoretical foundations and implementation architectures for space-filling curves, discrete global grids, and location coding schemes.

---

### Foundational and Mathematical Literature

* **Finkel, R. A., & Bentley, J. L.** (1974). Quad trees a data structure for retrieval on composite keys. *Acta Informatica*, 4(1), 1–9. DOI: [10.1007/BF00288933](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00288933)  
  *The foundational computer science paper introducing recursive 1-to-4 quadtree spatial partitioning.*

* **Gibb, R. G.** (2016). The rHEALPix discrete global grid system. *IOP Conference Series: Earth and Environmental Science*, 34(1), 012012. DOI: [10.1088/1755-1315/34/1/012012](https://doi.org/10.1088/1755-1315/34/1/012012)  
  *Formulates the Rearranged Hierarchical Equal Area isoLatitude Pixelization (rHEALPix) directly on the oblate WGS84/GRS80 reference ellipsoid.*

* **Gotsman, C., & Lindenbaum, M.** (1996). On the metric properties of discrete space-filling curves. *IEEE Transactions on Image Processing*, 5(5), 794–797. DOI: [10.1109/83.499920](https://doi.org/10.1109/83.499920)  
  *Proves the tight mathematical upper and lower dilation bounds ($L_2 \le 6$) for the locality preservation of the discrete Hilbert space-filling curve.*

* **Hales, T. C.** (2001). The Honeycomb Conjecture. *Discrete & Computational Geometry*, 25(1), 1–22. DOI: [10.1007/s004540010071](https://doi.org/10.1007/s004540010071)  
  *Rigorous mathematical proof that a regular hexagonal grid partitions the plane into equal-area cells with minimal total boundary perimeter.*

* **Hilbert, D.** (1891). Ueber die stetige Abbildung einer Linie auf ein Flächenstück. *Mathematische Annalen*, 38(3), 459–460. DOI: [10.1007/BF01199431](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01199431)  
  *The landmark publication introducing the continuous 2D Hilbert space-filling curve via recursive geometric quadrant reflections and rotations.*

* **Moon, B., Jagadish, H. V., Faloutsos, C., & Saltz, J. H.** (2001). Analysis of the clustering properties of the Hilbert space-filling curve. *IEEE Transactions on Knowledge and Data Engineering*, 13(1), 124–141. DOI: [10.1109/69.908985](https://doi.org/10.1109/69.908985)  
  *Derives the exact closed-form asymptotic number of contiguous 1D Hilbert index clusters required to cover arbitrary multidimensional query regions.*

* **Morton, G. M.** (1966). *A Computer Oriented Geodetic Data Base and a New Technique in File Sequencing*. Technical Report. Ottawa: IBM Canada Ltd.  
  *Introduced bit-interleaving coordinate mapping (Morton Z-order) for sequential geodetic database indexing in the Canada Geographic Information System (CGIS).*

* **Peano, G.** (1890). Sur une courbe, qui remplit toute une aire plane. *Mathematische Annalen*, 36(1), 157–160. DOI: [10.1007/BF01199438](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01199438)  
  *The historic discovery of the first base-3 continuous space-filling curve, proving that 1D continuous curves can map surjectively onto 2D areas.*

* **Sahr, K., White, D., & Kimerling, A. J.** (2003). Geodesic discrete global grid systems. *Cartography and Geographic Information Science*, 30(2), 121–134. DOI: [10.1559/152304003100011090](https://doi.org/10.1559/152304003100011090)  
  *Comprehensive review deriving the geometric and topological properties of polyhedral spherical tessellations, comparing icosahedral, octahedral, and cubic DGGS.*

* **Snyder, J. P.** (1992). An equal-area map projection for polyhedral globes. *Cartographica*, 29(1), 10–21. DOI: [10.3138/27H7-8K88-4882-1752](https://doi.org/10.3138/27H7-8K88-4882-1752)  
  *Derives the Icosahedral Snyder Equal-Area (ISEA) projection used in equal-area hexagonal and triangular global grids.*

### Open Standards and Modern DGGS Architectures

* **Brodsky, I.** (2018). H3: Uber's Hexagonal Hierarchical Spatial Index. *Uber Engineering Blog* (June 2018). Available at: `https://www.uber.com/blog/h3/`.  
  *The foundational architecture article detailing the design, Aperture 7 scaling, 12 pentagons, and C-core implementation of the Uber H3 grid system.*

* **International Organization for Standardization (ISO) & Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC).** (2021). *Geographic Information — Discrete Global Grid Systems Specifications — Part 1: Core Reference System and Operations, and Equal Area Earth Reference System*. ISO 19170-1:2021 / OGC Abstract Specification Topic 21. Geneva / Wayland, MA.  
  *The international open standard establishing the formal abstract specification, terminology, and functional requirements for Discrete Global Grid Systems.*

* **Rinckes, D., & Bunge, P.** (2014). *Open Location Code: An Open Source Standard for Addresses, Independent of Building Numbers and Street Names*. Google Zürich Engineering Technical Specification. GitHub repository: `https://github.com/google/open-location-code`.  
  *Defines the base-20 alphanumeric character set, hierarchical prefix scaling, and offline encoding algorithm for Plus Codes.*

* **Veach, E., et al.** (2011). *Google S2 Geometry Library*. Open-source C++ library and technical documentation. Available at: `https://s2geometry.io/`.  
  *Authoritative architectural documentation detailing the cube-to-sphere projection, non-linear coordinate warping, and 64-bit Hilbert quadtree indexing.*

* **Arthur, R.** (2023). A critical analysis of the What3Words geocoding algorithm. *PLOS ONE*, 18(10), e0292491. DOI: [10.1371/journal.pone.0292491](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0292491)  
  *Peer-reviewed mathematical and computational audit proving widespread homophone, plural, and single-edit confusability collisions within localized regions in the What3Words scheme.*
