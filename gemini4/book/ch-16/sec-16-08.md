*Indexing a spherical/ellipsoidal planet without polar singularities or projection seams—and the pitfalls of geocoding schemes.*

## 16.8 Curated Key References

The seminal mathematical papers, open standards specifications, and critical technical reports listed below establish the theoretical foundations and implementation architectures for space-filling curves, discrete global grids, and location coding schemes.

---

### Foundational and Mathematical Literature

* **Gotsman, C., & Lindenbaum, M.** (1996). On the metric properties of discrete space-filling curves. *IEEE Transactions on Image Processing*, 5(5), 794–797. DOI: [10.1109/83.495966](https://doi.org/10.1109/83.495966)  
  *Proves the tight mathematical upper and lower bounds for the 2D-to-1D locality preservation of the Hilbert space-filling curve.*

* **Hilbert, D.** (1891). Ueber die stetige Abbildung einer Linie auf ein Flächenstück. *Mathematische Annalen*, 38(3), 459–460. DOI: [10.1007/BF01199431](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01199431)  
  *The landmark publication introducing the continuous 2D Hilbert space-filling curve, establishing recursive geometric quadrant rotation.*

* **Morton, G. M.** (1966). *A Computer Oriented Geodetic Data Base and a New Technique in File Sequencing*. Technical Report. Ottawa: IBM Canada Ltd.  
  *Introduced bit-interleaving coordinate mapping (Morton Z-order) for geodetic database indexing.*

* **Peano, G.** (1890). Sur une courbe, qui remplit toute une aire plane. *Mathematische Annalen*, 36(1), 157–160. DOI: [10.1007/BF01199438](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01199438)  
  *The historic discovery of the first space-filling curve, proving that continuous curves can map onto higher-dimensional areas.*

* **Sahr, K., White, D., & Kimerling, A. J.** (2003). Geodesic discrete global grid systems. *Cartography and Geographic Information Science*, 30(2), 121–134. DOI: [10.1559/152304003100011090](https://doi.org/10.1559/152304003100011090)  
  *Comprehensive review deriving the geometric and topological properties of polyhedral spherical tessellations, comparing icosahedral, octahedral, and cubic DGGS.*

### Open Standards and Modern DGGS Architectures

* **Brodsky, I.** (2018). H3: A Hexagonal Hierarchical Spatial Index. *Uber Engineering Blog*. Available at: `https://www.uber.com/blog/h3/`.  
  *The foundational architecture paper detailing the design, Aperture 7 scaling, 12 pentagons, and C-core implementation of the Uber H3 grid system.*

* **International Organization for Standardization (ISO) & Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC).** (2021). *Geographic Information — Discrete Global Grid Systems — Part 1: Core Reference System and Operations, and Equal-Area Earth Grid Systems*. ISO 19170-1:2021 / OGC Topic 21. Geneva / Wayland, MA.  
  *The international open standard establishing the formal abstract specification, terminology, and functional requirements for Discrete Global Grid Systems.*

* **Rinckes, D., et al.** (2015). *Open Location Code: An Open Source Standard for Location Codes*. Google Zurich Technical Documentation. GitHub repository: `https://github.com/google/open-location-code`.  
  *Defines the base-20 alphanumeric character set, hierarchical prefix scaling, and offline encoding algorithm for Plus Codes.*

* **Veach, E., et al.** (2011). *Google S2 Geometry Library*. Open-source C++ library and technical documentation. Available at: `https://s2geometry.io/`.  
  *Authoritative architectural documentation detailing the cube-to-sphere projection, non-linear coordinate warping, and 64-bit Hilbert quadtree indexing.*

* **Woods, T.** (2021). *Why What3Words Is Not Suitable for Safety-Critical Communications*. Technical Report and Critical Analysis. Available at: `https://shkspr.mobi/blog/2021/04/what3words-the-algorithm/`.  
  *Independent computational audit documenting hundreds of near-homophone, plural, and typographic collision pairs in What3Words that map to adjacent regional jurisdictions.*
